# Investor Communications Skill

Maintain trust with investors and advisors through consistent, data-driven updates. Bad communication is worse than bad news — silence kills relationships.

## Core Principle

Every investor communication must do three things:
1. Show progress against the last update's commitments
2. Surface one honest challenge (not buried, not minimized)
3. Make a specific ask that leverages the investor's actual network or expertise

Investors who receive generic updates stop reading. Investors who receive honest, specific updates become active allies.

## Monthly Investor Update

### Cadence
- Send on the last business day of the month
- Set up a recurring calendar block — consistency signals operational maturity
- Use the same format every month so readers can scan to the section they care about

### Subject Line Format
```
[Company] — [Month YYYY] Update: [One headline stat or milestone]
```

Examples:
- `OASIS AI — March 2026 Update: MRR hit $2,982 (+11% MoM)`
- `PropFlow — March 2026 Update: First 50 waitlist signups`

### Email Template

```
Hi [Name],

Here's the March update. Short version at the top, detail below.

---

TL;DR
- WIN: [Biggest win this month — specific and quantified]
- METRIC: [Key number — MRR, clients, growth rate]
- CHALLENGE: [One honest challenge — no softening]

---

KEY METRICS
| Metric           | This Month | Last Month | Change |
|------------------|------------|------------|--------|
| MRR (USD)        | $X,XXX     | $X,XXX     | +X%    |
| ARR (USD)        | $XX,XXX    | $XX,XXX    | +X%    |
| Active Clients   | X          | X          | +X     |
| Pipeline (USD)   | $X,XXX     | $X,XXX     | —      |
| Burn Rate (USD)  | $XXX/mo    | $XXX/mo    | —      |
| Runway           | X months   | X months   | —      |

PRODUCT UPDATES

- [Feature or integration shipped]

What's in progress:
- [Initiative with expected completion date]

SALES & MARKETING
- New clients signed: [names or "N new clients" — get permission before naming]

---

(If solo: "Solo execution. First hire planned at $X MRR.")

---

ASKS

Generic asks ("any intros?") get ignored. Specific asks get answered.
```

## Pitch Deck Structure

### When to Build a Deck
- Approaching seed or pre-seed investors
- Applying to accelerators (YC, Techstars, Antler)
- Strategic partnership conversations requiring a formal presentation
- Internal alignment on company direction

### Slide-by-Slide Blueprint

**Slide 1 — Cover**
- Company name + logo
- One-line tagline (what you do for whom)
- Founder name + contact
- Date of version (decks go stale; date them)

**Slide 2 — Problem**
- The specific pain, not a vague market observation
- Use a real customer story or data point to anchor it
- Show you understand the problem better than anyone else in the room
- Avoid: "Businesses waste time on X" — be specific about who, how much, why it matters now

**Slide 3 — Solution**
- What your product does, shown (not described)
- One sentence: "[Product] helps [customer] do [outcome] by [mechanism]"
- Screenshot or demo GIF if the product exists
- Keep it visual — investors read decks at 10pm, not in optimal conditions

**Slide 4 — Market Size**
- TAM: Total addressable market (global, if realistic)
- SAM: Serviceable addressable market (your geography + segment)
- SOM: Your realistic 3-year capture
- Always show your methodology — "we estimated this by multiplying X × Y because Z"
- Red flag: citing a single market research report as your only source

**Slide 5 — Traction**
- MRR chart (month-over-month, minimum 3 data points)
- Client logos or count (with permission)
- Growth rate stated explicitly
- Key retention metric (churn rate or NRR if you have it)
- If pre-revenue: user signups, waitlist, pilot commitments, letters of intent

**Slide 6 — Business Model**
- Pricing tiers (show actual numbers, not "competitive pricing")
- Unit economics: CAC, LTV, LTV:CAC ratio, payback period
- How revenue scales with team size (is this a service or a product?)
- Gross margin (SaaS target: >70%; agency target: >50%)

**Slide 7 — Competition**
- 2×2 matrix or feature comparison table
- X axis: [differentiation dimension 1], Y axis: [differentiation dimension 2]
- Place yourself in the quadrant you own — be honest, not self-serving
- Name real competitors. "No direct competition" is a red flag.

**Slide 8 — Team**
- Founder(s): relevant experience, why you specifically can win this
- Key hires or advisors: name + 1-line credential
- Avoid: stock headshots, vague titles, listing everyone's university
- Include: any unfair advantages (domain expertise, existing network, prior exits)

**Slide 9 — Financials**
- P&L summary: last 12 months actual + next 24 months projected
- Revenue build: how you get from current MRR to projections (show the math)
- Key assumptions: hiring plan, CAC, churn, pricing changes
- Present 3 scenarios: base, bull, bear — shows you've pressure-tested the model

**Slide 10 — Ask**
- How much you are raising
- Instrument (SAFE, convertible note, priced round) and terms if set
- Use of funds (specific allocation: X% engineering, Y% sales, Z% marketing)
- Milestones unlocked: "This capital gets us to [metric] by [date]"
- Why now: what changes if you don't raise in the next 6 months

**Optional Slide 11 — Appendix**
- Full financials
- Technical architecture (for deep-tech)
- Customer testimonials
- Detailed market sizing methodology
- Cap table (share only when asked)

## Advisory Board Management

### Why Advisors Matter More Than Investors at Early Stage
Advisors give you access to networks and expertise without requiring dilution at scale. A well-chosen advisor introduces you to 5 clients — that compounds faster than a check.

### How to Recruit Advisors

**Offer structure:**
- Standard advisory equity: 0.1–0.5% vesting over 2 years (no cliff for advisors)
- Time commitment: 1-2 hours per month (quarterly call + async availability)
- Formalize with an advisor agreement (FAST Agreement template — standard in Canada/US)

**Target profile:**
- They have done what you want to do (exited, scaled, sold enterprise, etc.)
- They have networks you need (distribution channels, potential clients, investors)
- They are genuinely interested in your category — advisors who say yes to everything advise nobody well

**Recruitment approach:**
1. Warm introduction preferred — cold outreach works but takes 3× longer
2. First meeting is a "pick your brain" call, not a pitch
3. Propose the advisor relationship only after they've given useful input in a first conversation
4. Be specific: "I need help with enterprise sales in the HVAC vertical" is better than "looking for advisors"

### Advisor Update Cadence

**Monthly email (same format as investor update, shorter version):**
- One metric that moved
- One challenge you want their input on
- One specific ask (intro, advice, connection)

**Quarterly call (45 minutes):**
- 10 min: What happened since last call
- 20 min: The one thing you most need help with
- 10 min: What's changing, what you see coming
- 5 min: Clear action items with names and dates

**Rule:** Never have a call without a pre-sent agenda. Advisors with 10+ companies respect your preparation and protect their time accordingly.

### Extracting Maximum Value
- Always arrive with a specific question, not "any advice?"
- Send a 1-paragraph context brief before every call
- After the call, send a summary of what you discussed and what you're going to do
- Close every loop: if an advisor made an intro, report back what happened within 2 weeks

## Valuation Estimation

### When to Estimate Your Own Valuation
- Preparing for a raise (know what range is defensible before entering negotiations)
- Structuring an equity partnership or JV
- Issuing advisor equity (need a strike price for options)
- Annual financial planning (understanding personal equity value)

### Revenue Multiple Method (SaaS)

| Stage | ARR Range | Typical Multiple | Notes |
|-------|-----------|-----------------|-------|
| Pre-revenue | $0 | N/A — use comparable deal comps | Team + market + traction only |
| Pre-seed | <$100K ARR | 10–20× ARR | Largely founder/vision bet |
| Seed | $100K–$1M ARR | 10–15× ARR | Must show product-market fit signals |
| Series A | $1M–$5M ARR | 12–20× ARR | Growth rate and retention drive multiple |
| Series B | $5M–$20M ARR | 8–15× ARR | Unit economics scrutinized heavily |

**Formula:**
```
Estimated Valuation = ARR × Multiple
Conservative:  ARR × 8
Base:          ARR × 12
Optimistic:    ARR × 15 (requires strong growth + retention story)
```

**Adjustment factors (add or subtract 2-3× from base):**
- Growth rate >100% YoY: +2–3×
- Net Revenue Retention >110%: +2×
- Gross margin <50%: -2×
- Single-customer concentration >40%: -3×
- Churn >5%/month: -3×
- Proprietary data or distribution moat: +2×

### Comparable Company Analysis

When presenting to investors, support your multiple with 3-5 comparable deals:
1. Find 3-5 companies in your category that raised recently
2. Note their ARR at time of raise and the round valuation
3. Calculate implied multiple
4. Position your ask relative to comps (higher multiple requires a differentiating argument)

Sources: Crunchbase, PitchBook, VC Twitter, AngelList press releases.

### When to Get a Formal Valuation
- 409A valuation: required before issuing equity to employees in the US (get one from a firm like Carta or Pulley)
- Formal appraisal: required for M&A, due diligence, or cross-border partnerships
- Informal estimate: sufficient for advisor equity, early conversations, internal planning

## Partnership & Joint Venture Frameworks

### Partnership Evaluation Criteria

Before entering any formal partnership, score each dimension 1-5:

| Dimension | Questions to Answer |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Strategic fit | Does this partner reach customers we cannot reach alone? |
| Resource complementarity | Do they have what we lack (distribution, tech, capital)? |
| Revenue potential | What's the realistic 12-month revenue impact? |
| Risk exposure | What's our downside if this fails or they go dark? |
| Alignment | Do they have conflicting interests with us or our other clients? |
| Execution capacity | Can they actually deliver on commitments? |

Minimum score to proceed: 20/30. Below 20, the partnership is opportunistic, not strategic.

### Revenue Sharing Models

**Rev share (no equity):**
- Partner refers or co-delivers clients, earns X% of contract value
- Typical range: 10-20% for referrals, 20-40% for co-delivery
- Trigger: paid invoice, not signed contract
- Cap: consider a cap if the partner becomes too concentrated

**Equity swap:**
- Each party gives the other a small equity stake
- Aligns long-term incentives without cash changing hands
- Typical range: 0.5-2% of each company
- Requires board approval if your cap table has investors

**Licensing:**
- One party licenses technology or IP to the other
- Royalty model: X% of revenue derived from the licensed asset
- Flat fee model: annual or monthly fee regardless of usage

**White label:**
- Partner sells your product under their brand
- You get revenue, they get margin and a product to offer
- Requires strict SLA and support agreements

### Partnership Agreement Key Terms Checklist

Before signing any partnership agreement, verify these are addressed:

```
[ ] Scope definition — exactly what services/products are covered
[ ] Exclusivity — is this exclusive by geography, vertical, or client type?
[ ] Revenue share rate — percentage, calculation method, payment timing
[ ] Minimum performance — what happens if targets aren't met?
[ ] IP ownership — who owns co-developed IP?
[ ] Confidentiality — what can each party disclose to their own clients?
[ ] Term length — initial period and renewal mechanism
[ ] Termination for convenience — how much notice, what's owed on exit?
[ ] Non-solicitation — can the partner approach your clients directly after exit?
[ ] Liability cap — limits on what each party can be sued for
[ ] Governing law — which jurisdiction (Ontario, Canadian law, etc.)
```

### Exit Clauses and Sunset Provisions

Every partnership should have defined exit terms from day one:

- **Termination for convenience:** Either party exits with 30-60 days written notice
- **Termination for cause:** Immediate exit for material breach, fraud, or insolvency
- **Sunset provision:** If the partnership hasn't hit [metric] by [date], it automatically dissolves
- **Wind-down protocol:** What happens to active client accounts on exit — who takes ownership, how is billing transferred, what is the communication plan to clients?

Partnerships without exit terms create the worst outcomes: zombie relationships that drag on for years with no value to either party.
