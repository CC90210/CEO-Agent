# Sales Methodology — NEPQ Framework for OASIS AI

## Overview

CC's sales approach uses the Jeremy Miner NEPQ (Neuro-Emotional Persuasion Questions) framework. The core principle: questions sell better than pitching. Lead with their problem, not your product.

## The NEPQ Framework

### Phase 1: Connection (30 seconds)
**Goal:** Pattern interrupt. Sound different from every other salesperson.

Opening lines (choose one):
- "Hey [Name], I appreciate you taking the time. I know you're busy, so I'll be upfront — I'm not sure if what we do is even a fit for you. Mind if I ask a couple quick questions to find out?"
- "Before we dive in, I should mention — if at any point this doesn't seem relevant to what you're dealing with, just tell me and we'll wrap up. Fair enough?"

**Why this works:** It removes pressure. The prospect relaxes because you're not pushing.

### Phase 2: Situation Questions (2-3 minutes)
**Goal:** Understand their current state without assuming.

Questions:
- "Tell me about how you currently handle [relevant process] — walk me through your typical week."
- "When a new lead comes in, what happens next? Who handles it?"
- "How many hours a week would you say your team spends on [manual task]?"
- "What tools are you using right now for [function]?"

**Rules:**
- Listen more than you talk (80/20 rule)
- Take notes on specific numbers and pain points
- Don't jump to solutions yet

### Phase 3: Problem Awareness Questions (3-5 minutes)
**Goal:** Help them feel the pain of their current situation.

Questions:
- "What happens when [leads fall through the cracks / follow-ups get missed / response time is slow]?"
- "How much revenue would you estimate you're losing because of [specific problem they mentioned]?"
- "Has there been a time recently where [problem] cost you a deal or a client?"
- "On a scale of 1-10, how frustrated are you with [specific issue]?"

**Why this works:** People don't buy solutions to problems they don't feel. These questions make the pain tangible.

### Phase 4: Solution Awareness Questions (2-3 minutes)
**Goal:** Let them envision the solution before you present it.

Questions:
- "If you could wave a magic wand, what would [process] look like ideally?"
- "What would it mean for your business if [specific problem] was completely automated?"
- "If you could get back those [X hours/week], what would you do with that time?"
- "What would it be worth to you if [specific outcome they want]?"

### Phase 5: Consequence Questions (1-2 minutes)
**Goal:** Create urgency by exploring what happens if they don't act.

Questions:
- "What happens if you're still dealing with this 6 months from now?"
- "How does this affect your growth plans?"
- "What's the cost of doing nothing?"

**Caution:** Don't overdo this — one or two consequence questions is enough. More feels manipulative.

### Phase 6: Presentation (3-5 minutes)
**Goal:** Present your solution mapped specifically to their stated problems.

Structure:
1. "Based on what you've told me, here's what I think would work..."
2. Map each feature to a specific problem THEY mentioned (never generic features)
3. Use their language, not yours ("You mentioned X — here's how we solve X")
4. Give a specific example: "We did something similar for [similar business] and they saw [specific result]"
5. Present pricing tiers: "Most businesses like yours start at [tier], which includes [scope]"

### Phase 7: Objection Handling
Common objections and responses:

**"It's too expensive"**
- "I totally get that. Can I ask — what were you expecting the investment would be?"
- "Let me ask this differently — if the system saves you [X hours/week] and generates [Y additional revenue], would the ROI make sense?"

**"I need to think about it"**
- "Of course, take your time. Can I ask — what specifically are you weighing?"
- "Is there something I didn't cover that would help you make a decision?"

**"I'm not ready right now"**
- "When do you think would be the right time?"
- "What would need to change for this to become a priority?"

**"Can you send me something?"**
- "Absolutely. What specifically would be most helpful — a case study, pricing breakdown, or technical overview?"
- (Then send targeted material, not a generic brochure)

**"I want to compare with other options"**
- "That makes sense. What other solutions are you looking at?"
- "What criteria are most important to you in making this decision?"

### Phase 8: Close
**Goal:** Natural conclusion, not a hard close.

- "Based on everything we discussed, which package makes the most sense as a starting point?"
- "If we started this week, we could have [specific deliverable] ready by [date]. Does that timeline work?"
- "The next step would be [specific action]. Should we get that set up?"

**After verbal agreement:**
1. Send proposal within 24 hours (`/proposal`)
2. Include payment link (Stripe)
3. Follow SOP-013 (Proposal-to-Close Pipeline)

## Discovery Call Prep Checklist
Before every discovery call:
- [ ] Research the prospect's company (website, LinkedIn, reviews)
- [ ] Identify 3 potential pain points based on their industry
- [ ] Prepare 5 situation questions specific to their business
- [ ] Have relevant case study ready
- [ ] Know your pricing cold (no fumbling)
- [ ] Have calendar open for scheduling next steps

## Sales Metrics to Track
- Discovery calls booked per week (target: 3-5)
- Discovery-to-proposal conversion rate (target: 60%+)
- Proposal-to-close conversion rate (target: 40%+)
- Average deal size (target: $500-$2,000/mo)
- Sales cycle length (target: 14-21 days)
- Win/loss reasons (track in pipeline)
