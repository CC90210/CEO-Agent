# Scaling Playbook

The goal is not to work harder — it is to make each dollar of revenue require less of CC's direct time. Scaling is systematizing until the business can grow without you being the bottleneck.

## Core Principle

Every stage of growth has a specific bottleneck. If you solve the wrong bottleneck, nothing moves. Identify the current constraint, solve it completely, then move to the next one.

---

## Revenue-Based Scaling Triggers

Different MRR levels require different operating models. Acting like a $50K MRR company when you are at $3K MRR creates overhead that kills you. Acting like a $3K MRR company when you are at $25K MRR creates chaos.

| MRR | Stage | Primary Bottleneck | Key Actions |
|-----|-------|--------------------|-------------|
| $0–$2K | Survival | Finding product-market fit | Solo execution, close first 3 paying clients, do things that don't scale to learn what clients actually value |
| $2K–$5K | Stability | Systematizing what works | Document every delivery process as an SOP, build content engine for inbound, close the 4-5 clients needed for $5K |
| $5K–$10K | Growth | CC's time is the limit | Hire first support (VA or junior), automate admin (invoicing, scheduling, reporting), expand pipeline beyond referrals |
| $10K–$25K | Scale | Delivery quality at volume | Build team of 2-3, productize service (fixed scope, fixed price), raise prices for new clients, launch SaaS if applicable |
| $25K–$50K | Leverage | Management and coordination | Add management layer, pursue enterprise clients (higher ACV), build strategic partnerships, CC moves to sales and strategy only |
| $50K+ | Empire | Strategic capital allocation | Systems run without CC, focus shifts to new products, M&A, or raising capital for 10× growth |

### How to Know When You've Hit the Next Stage
You are ready for the next stage when the current bottleneck is solved AND the new bottleneck is starting to appear. The most common mistake is skipping stages — hiring before processes are documented, or building a team before the delivery model is repeatable.

## First Hire Decision Framework

The first hire is the highest-leverage decision in a solo company's lifecycle. Getting it right multiplies your output. Getting it wrong creates a management burden that makes you less productive than before.

### What to Hire First (Ranked by ROI)

**1. Virtual Assistant / Admin (highest ROI for solo founders)**
- What they do: email management, scheduling, CRM updates, research, basic content formatting, invoice sending, meeting prep
- What this frees: 8-15 hours/week of CC's time at $15-25/hour (versus CC's $200+/hour opportunity cost)
- Hire when: you are spending >5 hours/week on tasks that don't require your unique expertise
- Where to find: Upwork (Philippines VAs are excellent; budget $5-12/hour for general admin)
- Onboarding time: 1-2 weeks to train on your systems

**2. Business Development / Sales (high ROI if you have a proven offer)**
- What they do: outbound prospecting, lead research, initial outreach, follow-up sequences, CRM maintenance
- What this frees: 10-20 hours/week of pipeline work, letting CC focus only on closing calls
- Hire when: you have a repeatable offer that you can close consistently AND your pipeline work is eating your delivery time
- Compensation model: base ($2-3K/month) + commission (10-15% of first-year contract value)
- Where to find: LinkedIn, Contra, cold outreach to junior SDRs at SaaS companies

**3. Delivery / Technical (critical when quality is at risk)**
- What they do: execute client work (automation builds, technical integrations, content delivery)
- What this frees: CC's delivery time, allowing more clients without quality degradation
- Hire when: you have more client work than you can execute, OR a client has complained about turnaround time
- Where to find: Toptal (vetted developers), Upwork, local developer networks
- Red flag: hiring delivery before your SOPs exist means training every time — document first

**4. Marketing / Content (medium ROI, usually better to use systems than people)**
- What they do: content creation, scheduling, analytics, email campaigns
- What this frees: the content consistency burden
- Hire when: you have a clear content strategy and need execution, not strategy
- Alternative: build Bravo's content automation to the point where this hire is unnecessary until $20K+ MRR

### Full-Time vs. Contractor vs. Agency

| Model | Best For | Pros | Cons |
|-------|---------|------|------|
| Full-time employee | Long-term core roles (>30 hrs/week, ongoing) | Loyalty, culture, deep context | Fixed overhead, Canadian employment law obligations, benefits |
| Contractor | Project-based or specialized work | Flexible, no benefits, easy to scale up/down | Less integrated, potential IP issues, cannot control schedule |
| Agency | Specialized services (paid ads, PR, design) | Expertise on demand, no management | Expensive per unit, less responsive, no institutional knowledge |

**Canadian employment law note:** Misclassifying employees as contractors is a CRA risk. If someone works full-time, only for you, using your tools, on your schedule — they are likely an employee. Consult an employment lawyer before structuring any role.

## Service Productization Pathway

Moving from custom services to productized services to SaaS is a margin and scalability transformation. Every step increases your leverage.

### Level 1 — Custom Services
**Description:** Each client engagement is scoped and priced individually. High touch, high customization.
**Pricing model:** Time and materials or custom project quote
**Margin:** 40-60% (CC's time is the primary cost)
**Scalability:** Linear — more clients = proportionally more CC hours
**When to be here:** Early stage, still discovering what clients value most

### Level 2 — Templatized Services
**Description:** Delivery process is standardized internally (SOPs, templates, scripts) but appears custom to the client. Some customization at the edges.
**Pricing model:** Monthly retainer with defined deliverables
**Margin:** 55-70% (systems reduce delivery time)
**Scalability:** 1.5-2× — you can take on more clients without proportionally more hours
**When to be here:** Once you have 3+ clients getting the same core value

### Level 3 — Productized Service
**Description:** Fixed scope, fixed price, repeatable delivery. Clients buy a clearly defined outcome.
**Pricing model:** Packages (e.g., "Automation Starter — $1,500/month: includes X, Y, Z")
**Margin:** 65-80% (SOPs allow delegation)
**Scalability:** 2-3× — junior staff can execute the delivery
**When to be here:** Once delivery is well-documented and mostly repeatable

### Level 4 — Self-Serve Product
**Description:** Software product that clients use independently. No human delivery required.
**Pricing model:** SaaS subscription (monthly or annual)
**Margin:** 70-90% (server costs, no labor per client)
**Scalability:** Near-infinite — adding the 100th client costs almost nothing
**When to be here:** When you have enough clients to validate the product and capital or Bravo capacity to build it

### The Productization Steps (Level 1 → Level 3)
1. Document your three most common client engagements as SOPs
2. Identify which deliverables appear in all three (the common core)
3. Define the outcome clients consistently pay for
4. Package it: name, fixed scope, fixed price, fixed timeline
5. Test the package with the next 2 new clients — do they buy without customization?
6. If yes: retire custom quoting for this service type
7. If no: iterate the package until it sells without friction

## Pricing Strategy Evolution

Pricing strategy must evolve with the business. The pricing that gets you to $5K MRR will not get you to $25K MRR.

### Solo Stage ($0–$5K MRR): Value-Based Pricing
- Price based on the outcome delivered, not hours spent
- Quote custom based on each client's situation
- Starting point for OASIS AI retainers: $1,500-$3,000 USD/month
- Never price based on your costs — price based on the value you create
- If 80% of prospects say yes immediately, you are priced too low

### Team Stage ($5K–$20K MRR): Tiered Packages
- Three packages: Starter / Growth / Scale (or Good / Better / Best)
- Each package has a defined scope, defined deliverables, defined price
- Middle tier should account for 60-70% of sales (the "Goldilocks" effect)
- Annual plans: offer 10-15% discount for annual prepay (improves cash flow)
- Price anchor: always show the highest tier first

**Example OASIS AI package structure:**
| Tier | Price | Deliverables |
|------|-------|-------------|
| Starter | $1,500 USD/mo | 1 automation workflow, monthly check-in, email support |
| Growth | $2,500 USD/mo | 3 automation workflows, bi-weekly calls, Slack access, reporting dashboard |
| Scale | $4,500 USD/mo | Unlimited workflows, weekly calls, dedicated support, custom integrations |

### Scale Stage ($20K+ MRR): Published Pricing + Self-Serve
- Public pricing page (reduces time spent on pricing conversations)
- Self-serve onboarding for lower tiers (no sales call required)
- Enterprise custom pricing for deals >$5K/month (requires sales process)
- Annual contracts standard above $3K/month (creates predictability)

### Common Pricing Mistakes to Avoid
- Hourly billing: creates a ceiling on your earnings and incentivizes inefficiency
- Matching competitor pricing without understanding their cost structure
- Discounting to close: creates a bad precedent and attracts the wrong clients
- Never raising prices: you will be trapped at your first year's rates forever
- Charging less than you need to deliver the quality you've promised

### When and How to Raise Prices
- Raise prices on new clients immediately when you are at >80% capacity
- Raise prices on existing clients with 60 days notice and a clear value justification
- Grandfathering existing clients: acceptable for 6 months, then they move to new pricing
- Price increase framing: "We're investing in [specific improvement]. New pricing reflects that."

## Operations Scaling Checklist

Before adding headcount or capacity, ensure your operations can support scale without breaking.

```
[ ] All client delivery processes documented as SOPs in memory/SOP_LIBRARY.md
[ ] Client onboarding under 5 days (contract → kickoff call with first deliverable started)
[ ] Billing fully automated via Stripe subscriptions (no manual invoicing)
[ ] Client reporting automated (no manual email updates — dashboards or automated reports)
[ ] Quality assurance gate defined (what gets reviewed before it goes to a client, and by whom)
[ ] Emergency protocol exists (what happens if CC is unreachable for 48 hours?)
[ ] Client communication SLAs defined (response time standards, who responds to what)
[ ] Offboarding process defined (how a client leaves cleanly, what data is returned)
[ ] Contractor agreements signed for all non-employee workers
[ ] Accounting up to date (can you answer "what is your profit this month?" in under 60 seconds?)
```

### Emergency Protocol (48-Hour Absence Plan)
Define this before you need it. At minimum:
- Client contacts: who do clients call if CC is unreachable?
- Active deliverables: what is in flight and who can update the client on status?
- Payments: are subscriptions automated so clients aren't disrupted?
- Critical access: does anyone else have emergency access to critical accounts?
- Communication: is there a single message CC can send to all clients that explains the delay?
