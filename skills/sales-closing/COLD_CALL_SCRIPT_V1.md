# OASIS Cold Call Script — V1.0

> Five stages. Under 3 minutes. NEPQ-aligned. Paired with the new free-pilot deal architecture (`brain/DEAL_ARCHITECTURE.md`). Memorize, don't read. Drill daily.

## When to Use
- Cold calling service businesses (HVAC, dental, landscaping, plumbing, chiropractic, real estate)
- Outbound to professional services (accountants, fractional CFOs, marketing agencies — though for these prefer the *partner* pitch from DEAL_ARCHITECTURE.md, not the client pitch)
- Any first-touch phone outbound where the prospect doesn't know who CC is yet

## When NOT to Use
- Warm leads who already booked / replied — different framework (use `skills/sales-closing/SKILL.md` discovery flow)
- Strategic partner outreach — use the partner recruitment pitch from `brain/DEAL_ARCHITECTURE.md`
- Inbound calls — they came to you, don't run pattern interrupt; jump straight to diagnose

## Stage 1 — Pattern Interrupt (~15 sec)

> "Hey [Name], it's Conaugh — we haven't spoken before, this is a cold call. You can hang up, or give me 30 seconds to tell you why I'm calling. Fair?"

**Mechanics:** disarm + permission. Removes "salesy" energy. ~80% say "fair" out of curiosity. The 20% who hang up wouldn't have bought anyway.

## Stage 2 — The Reason (~20 sec)

> "I work with [their niche] businesses around [region], and a lot of them are losing 8 to 15 hours a week to admin work — booking, follow-ups, lead chasing. I'm not sure if that's something you've thought about, but I figured you'd be the right person to ask."

**Mechanics:** "I'm not sure if..." is the NEPQ disarm — a question dressed as a statement. Forces the prospect to think. The second they're thinking, they're engaged.

## Stage 3 — Diagnose (~90 sec, 5 questions)

Ask one at a time. Listen. Do not interrupt. Do not pitch.

1. "Walk me through how your team currently handles [process]. Just so I understand."
2. "How long does that take per week, ballpark?"
3. "Have you looked at any tools or AI for it before? What stopped you?"
4. "If that just… happened in the background and you got those hours back — what would you actually do with the time?"
5. **Consequence question:** "And if nothing changes — same setup 12 months from now — what does that look like for the business?"

**Critical:** Q5 is where they sell themselves. Do NOT skip. Do NOT soften. Ask it. Wait. Let silence work.

## Stage 4 — The Pivot (~30 sec)

> "Here's what I do differently from anyone else you've probably talked to. I don't ask you to pay anything up front. We pick one process — the most painful one — we build the AI for it, and you run it for 14 days. If it saves you the time and money we projected, you pay implementation plus a small monthly to keep it running. If it doesn't, you owe me nothing and I walk. I'm that confident in the work. Worth a 15-minute walkthrough this week to see if it's a fit?"

**Mechanics:** transfers risk onto OASIS. The "no" gets very hard to say. This is the deal architecture (Offer 1) in script form.

**Variant for custom-software prospects** (Jonathan-style): replace "we build the AI for it, and you run it for 14 days" with "we run a free strategy session, scope a fixed-price build, and you only pay 50% deposit when you're 100% bought into the scope."

**Variant for C-suite consulting prospects:** "We do a free 30-minute pitch call where I show you exactly what the AI strategy roadmap would look like for your business. You walk away with a deliverable either way."

## Stage 5 — The Close (~15 sec)

> "I've got [day 1] at [time 1] or [day 2] at [time 2] open. Which works?"

**Critical:** never "let me know what works" or "send me your availability." Always two specific slots. Binary choice processes 4× faster than open-ended ask.

## Total Runtime: 2:50–3:10

If running over 4 minutes → cut Stage 3 talking. Prospect should be 65%+ of the speech by mid-Stage-3.

## Objection Handlers (8 most common)

| Trigger | Response |
|---------|----------|
| "Send me info." | "Yeah I can do that — but the info I send only makes sense after 5 minutes on the phone, otherwise it's just noise. What's the actual concern, that we're not legit, or that AI feels too early for you?" |
| "Too expensive / no budget." | "I haven't quoted you anything yet. So is it that you're worried it won't be worth what we charge, or that you can't justify any new spend right now no matter what?" |
| "We have someone." | "Smart, that's a sign you're growth-oriented. Out of curiosity — are they doing AI specifically, or running ads/marketing? Different categories." |
| "Not interested." | "Totally fair. Out of curiosity though — is it that AI feels too early, or you've tried something before and it didn't deliver? I'm trying to figure out what people actually need." |
| "Bad time." | "Got it. When's a better time — a week, a month, never? I'd rather know now than chase you for nothing." |
| "I'll think about it." | "Of course. What's the part you actually want to think about — the technology, the cost, the timing, or whether we're the right people?" |
| "Too good to be true." | "I get that a lot — that's exactly why I do it free for 14 days. There's literally no scenario where you lose money. The risk is on me, not you." |
| "How does it work?" | "Quick: we pick one process, build the AI, hand it over, you run it 14 days. We measure together on day 14. If it saved you what we promised, you pay implementation plus monthly. If not, walk." |

**Universal rule:** every objection → question, don't answer. The objection is rarely the real reason. Get them to articulate the real reason; the deal opens up.

## Drill Protocol (Daily)

1. **Mirror Run** — 5 min, morning. Stand. Run full script aloud.
2. **Objection Volley** — 10 min, midday. Trigger → 1.5-second pause → response. Speed up each round.
3. **Recording Review** — 10 min, evening. Listen back to one real call at 1.5×. Note one fix.

Daily for 90 days. Most people quit at week 3 because early data is bad. Data turns at week 5, incredible by week 9. The discipline is the moat.
