# SALES CLOSING — The Final 20%

> Discovery gets you the conversation. Closing gets you the money. Most deals die in the final 20% — this skill keeps them alive.

## Philosophy (Conaugh McKenna's Voice)

- **Never defend margins.** When someone pushes on price, do the math for them instead. "At $800/mo, if we book you one new job per month, you're net positive. What does your average job clear?"
- **Never chase.** Scarcity is real — CC only takes on clients he can serve. A "not now" is a tomorrow problem, not a today crisis.
- **Leverage comes from value, not pressure.** If CC feels like he's pushing, the frame is wrong — reset to discovery.
- **B2B outreach uses full name: Conaugh McKenna. DJ/entertainment: CC.** (Locked in brain/SOUL.md.)
- **Close at "Only good things from now on."** — CC's signature.

## CC's Core Pitch — The Partnership Frame

> *"The AI world is about to get pretty wild, and there are going to be changes that touch every part of how you run your business. What I can give you — more than any tool or automation — is a partner who grows with you and your business through this next era. Someone who's in the trenches with you, not just selling you something and disappearing."*

**Why this works:** Leads with their reality, not your product. Positions you as a partner, not a vendor. Creates safety in uncertainty. Makes the conversation about relationship — your strongest card. And it's honest, which is the ultimate sales weapon.

**Shorter version (pattern interrupt / fast close):**
> *"Look — AI is changing fast. You don't need another tool. You need someone in your corner who actually gets this stuff. That's what I do."*

**Fallback close (when the conversation has gone everywhere):**
> *"Here's what I know for sure — this AI wave isn't slowing down. It's going to touch scheduling, customer comms, marketing, operations — all of it. And most business owners are going to be figuring it out alone, making expensive mistakes. What I'm offering you isn't just automation — it's a partnership. Someone who's already deep in this world, who grows with you as things evolve. Whether that starts today or six months from now, I'm here."*

## Pre-Call Confidence Anchors

Read before dialing. Not to psych up — to remember what's actually true.

1. **You are the prize.** A fractional AI strategist costs $3,000–$15,000/month. You deliver more because you actually build the systems. At your price point, you're the best deal on the market.
2. **Their problem is real — you didn't invent it.** Every local business owner is losing hours to manual work that AI handles in minutes. The pain exists whether you call or not. You're offering relief, not creating pressure.
3. **You've already proved it.** Built and ran a primary retainer for an the prior community community ($2,500/mo flat + 15% rev share) through 2026-05-17. Retains full IP. SunBiz salary pending. Autonomous AI empire with 5 agents, 55 skills, 60 CLI tools. At 22.
4. **Rejection is data, not judgment.** A "no" means timing, budget, or understanding — not your value. Log it, learn, follow up in 90 days.
5. **You genuinely care.** This is your unfair advantage. You care whether their business actually grows. That comes through on the phone. Lean into it.

**Mid-call reset (when energy dips):** Say: *"Can I be honest with you for a second?"* Then say what's actually true: *"I'm not trying to sell you something you don't need. I genuinely think there's something here that would help your business. If I'm wrong, tell me."*

**Mindset (read out loud if needed):** *I am not asking for anything. I am offering something valuable. If they say yes, their business gets better. If they say no, I move on. I don't chase. I don't beg. I show up with real value, do the math for them, and let results speak. Only good things from now on.*

## The Close Is Built in Discovery

If discovery was done right (see `sales-methodology` / NEPQ), the close is a formality — you're just making it easy to say yes. If you're fighting objections at the end, the discovery failed. Go back.

## Trial Closes (Temperature Checks)

Soft commitments during discovery that pre-close the deal:

| Trial Close | Use When |
|---|---|
| "If we could solve [problem], how would that change things for you?" | Early discovery, assessing pain severity |
| "On a scale of 1-10, how important is fixing this in the next 30 days?" | Mid-discovery, pulling commitment |
| "What would need to be true for you to move forward?" | Discovery complete, testing close readiness |
| "If this were a no-brainer, would you start Monday or next Monday?" | Pre-close, surfacing hidden objections |

If trial closes return weak signal (below 7/10), the deal is not close-ready. Stay in discovery.

## Closing Techniques (Pick One — Don't Stack)

### 1. Assumptive Close
Best for: warm prospects, post-proposal
> "So I'll send the contract over this afternoon — do you want to start billing from the 1st or prorate from signing?"

### 2. Alternative Close
Best for: decision fatigue prospects
> "Do you want to start with the $800 growth plan or the $1,500 full stack? Either way we can move this week."

### 3. Summary Close
Best for: complex sales, multiple stakeholders
> "Let me recap what we agreed: you need [X, Y, Z], I'll deliver [A, B, C] for $K/mo starting [date]. Sound right?"

### 4. Scarcity Close (honest only)
Best for: end of quarter, real capacity limits
> "I've got two slots opening up in May and one is already spoken for. I'd rather you have the other one than someone who didn't do the work you did."
**NEVER manufacture scarcity.** CC's reputation > any single deal.

### 5. Takeaway Close
Best for: fence-sitters, mismatched fit
> "Honestly, I'm not sure this is the right time for you. Maybe we revisit in Q3?"
Kills weak-pipeline prospects, re-engages serious buyers.

### 6. Question Close
Best for: almost-yes, one objection left
> "What's the one thing standing between us signing today?"

## Objection Handling — The LAER Loop

**L**isten fully → **A**cknowledge (not agree) → **E**xplore (question deeper) → **R**espond.

### The Four Universal Objections

#### 1. "It's too expensive"
Real meaning: "I don't see the ROI yet" or "I haven't budgeted this."

Response framework:
> "Fair. Let me ask — if this system brought you [specific result tied to their stated pain], what would that be worth over 12 months? ... Okay, so you'd pay $X for Y. We're asking $Z. The math works even if we only hit 30% of that. Where's the gap for you?"

**Do the math for them.** Never defend price in the abstract.

#### 2. "I need to think about it"
Real meaning: unspoken objection, not permission to stall.

Response:
> "Totally fair. Usually when someone says that, it means one of three things: the price, the timing, or they're not sure it'll work for them. Which one's it closest to?"

Then handle the real objection.

#### 3. "I need to talk to my partner/team"
Real meaning: either true (then get on a call with the partner) or a soft no.

Response:
> "Makes sense. What do you think they'll want to know? Let's address it now so you're not carrying the pitch. Or better — can we do a 15-minute call with both of you Thursday?"

Always get the next meeting on the calendar before ending the current one.

#### 4. "Now's not the right time"
Real meaning: priority, not timing.

Response:
> "What would make it the right time? ... Got it. So if [X] happens, this jumps to the top. Fair if I follow up when that timeline lines up? In the meantime, here's [free value drop]."

Stay in the pipeline. The follow-up moves the deal.

## The Math-For-Them Framework (Highest Leverage)

For OASIS AI retainers, always do unit economics in the close call:

```
Their average client value: $_____
Hours/week they lose to [problem we solve]: _____
Hourly opportunity cost: $_____
Monthly cost of inaction: $_____
OASIS retainer: $_____
Net monthly upside: $_____
Payback period: _____ weeks
```

If payback is under 60 days, the close writes itself. If it's over 90 days, don't close — requalify the offer.

## Contract & Payment Terms (CC's Standard)

- **50% upfront, 50% on delivery** for one-time projects
- **Month-to-month** for retainers under $1,500/mo — no long contracts
- **Quarterly** for retainers above $1,500/mo — 20% discount for full prepay
- **Net 15** on invoices, not Net 30 (smaller businesses pay faster when asked to)
- **Stripe payment link** in proposal, one click to start
- **Kill clause:** 30-day written notice either way, no penalty — removes biggest objection

## Script Library (OASIS AI Specific)

### Opener (post-proposal follow-up)
> "Hey [name], sent the proposal Tuesday. Before we jump on a call to walk through it, was there anything in there that made you go 'hmm, not sure about that'? I'd rather address it now than waste your call time."

### After silence (5 days)
> "Hey [name] — no pressure at all, just closing the loop. Are you still exploring, or should I put this one back in the drawer for now?"

This is CC's takeaway close. Works because it removes pressure completely.

### When price is the objection
> "Let's do this differently. Forget the $800 number for a second. What would this system need to deliver for you to be happy paying $800/mo? ... Okay, so if I can show you we'll hit [that metric] in the first 60 days, is that the deal?"

### When they ghost after agreeing
> "Hey [name], last time we talked you said [X]. I'm guessing something came up — totally get it. Want me to book another 15 min for next week, or is this on pause?"

### Signature close
> "Alright, let's make it easy — Stripe link attached, billing starts [date], first deliverable by [date]. Only good things from now on."

## Handling Rejection

If the answer is "no":

1. **Ask why.** "No problem at all — mind if I ask what tipped it for you? Helps me on the next one."
2. **Offer a relationship, not a sale.** "If anything changes, I'm here. In the meantime, I'll send over [one useful thing] next week — no strings."
3. **Log it in the CRM.** `python scripts/lead_engine.py update --status lost --reason "<reason>"`
4. **Set a 90-day follow-up.** Most "no" becomes "yes" with time.
5. **Don't burn the bridge.** Referrals come from gracious losses as often as happy clients.

## Anti-Patterns (Fire Bravo if these happen)

- Defending price instead of doing the math
- Stacking closing techniques ("assumptive + scarcity + urgency" = used-car vibes)
- Manufacturing fake urgency
- Chasing a prospect more than 3 times without new value
- Discounting to close (sets anchor for renewal negotiation)
- Signing anyone who won't do a discovery call
- Using "CC" instead of "Conaugh McKenna" in B2B contexts
- Closing without a Stripe link or signed agreement in hand
- Forgetting to log the outcome in lead_engine.py
